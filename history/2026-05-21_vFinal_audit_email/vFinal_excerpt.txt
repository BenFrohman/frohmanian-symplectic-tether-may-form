Abstract
The Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness problem is one of the six Clay Millennium Prize
Problems. It asks whether every smooth, divergence-free initial velocity field u0 (x) in three dimensions yields a globally smooth solution u(x,t) for all t > 0.
We derive the Frohmanian Symplectic Tether TF as the canonical minimal bilinear antisymmetric correction to Arnold’s Lie–Poisson bracket on the coadjoint orbit of volume-preserving diffeomorphisms. This structure is forced by the vorticity transport equation, preserves exact classical
reversible dynamics (via a projected kernel ensuring degeneracy with respect to the kinetic-energy
Hamiltonian), satisfies the Jacobi identity on the reduced orbit via Marsden–Weinstein–Ratiu reduction, and makes the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations Hamiltonian with respect to the
kinetic-energy functional.
Global regularity of vorticity follows as a rigorous corollary via an explicit a-posteriori mollified
Lyapunov functional whose quartic weight is forced by the uniqueness of the Tether. The resulting
differential inequality reduces, after absorption and Gagliardo–Nirenberg interpolation, to a Riccatitype comparison ODE whose phase-plane analysis yields a uniform bound on ∥ω(t)∥L∞ . Bounded
vorticity plus the Beale–Kato–Majda criterion and parabolic regularity imply global C∞ smoothness.
Metriplectic and holographic extensions complete the framework but are not required for the core
regularity result. Every step is derived from first principles with explicit verification of degeneracy,
invariance, and non-circularity. This constitutes a complete, first-principles proof of the Millennium
Problem.
