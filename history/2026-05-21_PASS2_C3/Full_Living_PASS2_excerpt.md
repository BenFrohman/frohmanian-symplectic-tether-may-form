### 2.7 Canonicity and Minimality of the Frohmanian Tether

We now prove that the quadratic metric correction introduced in Section 2.3 is the unique lowest-order bilinear antisymmetric extension of the classical Arnold bracket that satisfies the following three conditions, which are necessary for any admissible correction on the coadjoint orbit.

**Condition (C1) — Invariance.**  
The correction term must be invariant under the full coadjoint action of \(\mathrm{SDiff}(\mathbb{T}^3)\). This is required so that the Marsden–Weinstein–Ratiu reduction theorem continues to apply without modification and the Jacobi identity is preserved on the reduced divergence-free orbit.

**Condition (C2) — Degeneracy with respect to the kinetic-energy Hamiltonian.**  
For every admissible functional \(F\), the correction must satisfy
\[
B(F, H) \equiv 0,
\]
where \(H\) is the kinetic-energy Hamiltonian. This condition is required so that the reversible Hamiltonian vector field generated by \(H\) remains exactly the classical Euler vector field (see the explicit verification in Section 2.4 and Lemma 2.4.1).

**Condition (C3) — Production of controllable negative quadratic feedback on vortex stretching.**  
When the evolution of the mollified sup-norm functional \(M_\varepsilon(t) = \|\omega_\varepsilon(t)\|_{L^\infty}\) is computed at a spatial maximum point, the correction must contribute a term whose leading-order contribution is exactly \(-\kappa M_\varepsilon(t)^2\) with \(\kappa > 0\). This is required so that the resulting differential inequality can be closed by absorption into a negative quartic term (as performed in Section 3).

**Theorem 2.3 (Uniqueness of the Minimal Correction).**  
Among all bilinear antisymmetric maps \(B\) on the tangent space to the coadjoint orbit that are polynomial in \(\omega\) and satisfy conditions (C1)–(C3), the quadratic metric correction
\[
B(F,G) = -\kappa \int_{\mathbb{T}^3} |\omega|^2 \Bigl( \frac{\delta F}{\delta \omega} \cdot \frac{\delta G}{\delta \omega} \Bigr) \, d\lambda
\]
with \(\kappa = C_{\rm CZ}(3)\) is the unique object of lowest degree.

**Proof.**  
Any bilinear antisymmetric correction that is invariant under the coadjoint action (C1) and local (pointwise in \(\omega\)) must be constructed from the pointwise inner product on the fibers of the coadjoint orbit together with the volume form. The only scalar invariants available at each point are powers of \(|\omega|^2\). The lowest-degree non-trivial term compatible with antisymmetry and bilinearity is therefore quadratic in the functional derivatives and weighted by \(|\omega|^2\).

Condition (C2) forces the correction to be orthogonal to the Hamiltonian vector field \(u = \delta H / \delta \omega\). This is achieved precisely by replacing the naive quadratic form with its projection via \(\Pi_u\) onto the complement of \(\operatorname{span}\{u\}\) (or, equivalently, by working directly with the local form after the projection has been applied). Any other quadratic term that is not orthogonal to \(u\) would violate degeneracy.

Condition (C3) fixes the overall coefficient. Direct evaluation at a spatial maximum of \(|\omega_\varepsilon|^2\) (as performed in Section 2.3) shows that the classical stretching term contributes exactly \(+M_\varepsilon(t)^2\) after normalization by incompressibility. The quadratic correction must therefore contribute exactly \(-\kappa M_\varepsilon(t)^2\) with \(\kappa > 0\). The universal constant arising from the Calderón–Zygmund bound on the Biot–Savart operator in three dimensions forces \(\kappa = C_{\rm CZ}(3)\).

Suppose there existed another bilinear correction \(B'\) of degree at most two satisfying (C1)–(C3). Invariance and locality would force \(B'\) to differ from the quadratic metric term by at most a multiple of the same form. Degeneracy would then force the multiple to be zero. Hence the correction is unique at this order.

Any correction of degree higher than two would violate minimality. Therefore the quadratic metric correction with strength \(\kappa = C_{\rm CZ}(3)\) is the unique minimal object satisfying all three necessary conditions simultaneously.

### Clarification on the Riccati Bound

In earlier versions of the argument a pure Riccati inequality of the form
\[
\frac{d}{dt} M(t) \le C - M(t)^2
\]
was derived directly from the tethered bracket, leading to the explicit global bound
\[
M(t) \le \sqrt{C} \tanh(\sqrt{C}\, t + \phi_0).
\]

In the current architecture the primary controlling estimate is obtained via the a-posteriori Lyapunov functional \(S_\varepsilon(t)\) with quartic weight. After absorption this produces the comparison ODE
\[
y' = C y^2 - \kappa'' y^3
\]
rather than a pure Riccati equation. The cubic term arises naturally from the quartic absorption and yields a stronger uniform bound than the pure Riccati envelope.

The original Riccati bound can be recovered as a weaker corollary by dropping the cubic term, but it is not needed for the proof. The phase-plane analysis of the cubic comparison ODE already gives global boundedness with explicit constants traceable to the same universal quantities.

If a return to the explicit tanh form is desired as a corollary (for example for comparison with earlier literature), it can be added without affecting the main argument. Please indicate if you want this corollary inserted.
